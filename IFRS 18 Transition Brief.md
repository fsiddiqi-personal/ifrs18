# IFRS 18 Transition Brief: From IAS 1 to the New Presentation Standard

Nov 22, 2026 ·  Sohail

## Why it matters now

IFRS 18 *Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements* replaces IAS 1 for annual periods beginning on or after **1 January 2027**. It does not change recognition or measurement, but it changes how performance is presented and explained, and it applies **retrospectively**. For December year-ends, the **2026 comparatives must be restated**, and the new presentation also applies to the first 2027 interim report under IAS 34. Early adoption is permitted, and local endorsement timing varies by jurisdiction. The 2026 financial statements should already include the IAS 8 disclosure of the expected impact.

## What changes: three pillars

**1. A structured profit or loss statement.** Income and expenses are classified into five categories: *operating, investing, financing, income taxes* and *discontinued operations*. Two subtotals become mandatory: **operating profit** and **profit before financing and income taxes**. Entities whose main business activity is investing or financing customers (banks, insurers, investment entities) classify some items differently.

**2. Management-defined performance measures (MPMs).** Adjusted EBITDA, underlying profit and similar measures that are used in public communications must be disclosed in a single note. That note explains why each measure is useful, reconciles it to the nearest IFRS subtotal, and shows the tax and non-controlling interest effect of each reconciling item.

**3. Aggregation and disaggregation.** New principles govern how items are grouped and labelled. Vague "other" balances are restricted. Entities that present expenses by function must disclose specified expenses by nature in the notes: depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, impairment and inventory write-downs.

**Consequential IAS 7 changes.** Operating profit becomes the starting point of the indirect cash flow method, and most of the choices for classifying interest and dividends are removed.

## Transition roadmap

| Phase | Timing (Dec year-end) | Key actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Diagnose | Now | Map the chart of accounts to the new categories; inventory all MPMs used externally; assess main business activities at group and subsidiary level |
| 2. Design | Q4 2026 | Draft pro forma P&L and notes; decide between nature and function presentation; set an MPM policy and governance |
| 3. Build | Q4 2026 – Q1 2027 | Add category tags in the ERP or consolidation tool; update reporting packs; capture by-nature data for function-based P&Ls |
| 4. Restate | By first 2027 interim | Re-present 2026 comparatives; prepare the required line-by-line reconciliation from IAS 1 to IFRS 18 for the comparative period |
| 5. Communicate | Throughout | Brief the board, auditors, lenders and investors; complete the IAS 8 impact disclosure in the 2026 financial statements |

## Common pitfalls

- **Foreign exchange and derivative gains** follow the category of the item they relate to, which needs mapping below account level.
- **Share of profit from associates and joint ventures** moves to the investing category, outside operating profit.
- **Loan covenants, bonus schemes and KPIs** that refer to "operating profit" or EBITDA may need redefining or renegotiating.
- **MPM reconciliations** require tax and NCI effects per adjustment, and that data is often not tracked today.
- **Group consistency:** subsidiaries with different main business activities can produce different classifications on consolidation.

## Recommended next step

Run a short gap assessment on the latest annual report: re-cast the P&L into the five categories, list every MPM, and flag system and covenant impacts. That gives a costed plan before the 2026 year-end close.
